# AI careers

The main roles in AI, what each one does day to day, and where to start in this repo. Titles vary between companies, so read the job description, not just the title.

**Roles:** anyone starting out or switching into AI.
**Prerequisites:** none

## Roles at a glance

| Role | What you do | Core skills | Start here |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI engineer | Build products on top of LLMs: prompts, RAG, agents, evals | Python, APIs, prompting, retrieval, evaluation | [AI engineer path](../../README.md#ai-engineer-building-llm-apps-and-agents) |
| ML engineer | Train, fine-tune, deploy and operate models | Python, PyTorch, ML fundamentals, MLOps, cloud | [ML engineer path](../../README.md#ml-engineer-training-adapting-and-deploying-models) |
| Data scientist | Answer business questions with data, experiments and models | Statistics, SQL, Python, classic ML, communication | [Data scientist path](../../README.md#data-scientist-from-data-to-models) |
| Research scientist or engineer | Invent and test new models and methods | Math, deep learning, reading and writing papers | [Foundations](../../01-foundations/), then [Adapting models](../../03-adapting-models/) |
| MLOps or platform engineer | Run the infrastructure for training and serving | Kubernetes, cloud, CI/CD, GPUs, observability | [Ops and deployment](../../09-ops-and-deployment/) |
| AI product manager | Decide what to build, define success, ship responsibly | Product sense, evals, cost and risk tradeoffs | [PM and leader path](../../README.md#product-manager-or-business-leader-whats-possible-and-what-it-takes) |
| AI security or governance specialist | Protect AI systems and keep them compliant | Security, risk management, regulation | [AI security](../../08-evaluation-and-safety/ai-security/), [AI governance](../../08-evaluation-and-safety/ai-governance/) |

## Key concepts

- **Fundamentals still matter.** Python, SQL, git, statistics and a working knowledge of machine learning carry over between roles and outlast any framework.
- **Build a portfolio, not a certificate collection.** Ship small end-to-end projects that someone can try: a deployed app, a repo with a README, and a short write-up of your decisions and how you evaluated the result. Courses are a means, not the proof. [My projects](../../projects/) show the idea.
- **Show evaluation.** Demos are easy. Showing that you measured quality, found failures and fixed them is what sets candidates apart.
- **Specialize gradually.** Start broad, then go deep in one area (for example retrieval, agents, serving or evaluation) where you can show real work.
- **Keep learning, selectively.** The field moves fast. Follow a few good sources, read the occasional paper, and rebuild something you use to understand how it works.
